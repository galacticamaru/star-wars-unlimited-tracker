A: Two columns, stepper bar under the art

Trades one grid column for control space. The stepper is a permanent part of the tile — no hover, no overlay, nothing invisible. This is the mobile-commerce pattern applied directly.

The measurement

At 390px with 2 columns: tile ≈ 183px. The stepper needs 44 + 44 = 88px of buttons plus a count label. Fits with room

Trade-offs

  • Rapid-fire: good — every card's + is one tap, always visible, no selection step.
  • Art forward: best of the three — art is large and legible at 183px wide.
  • Precise: good — 44px targets with real separation.
  • Cost: roughly 4 cards visible per screen instead of 9. More scrolling to browse.

Build cost

  • useColumnCount() base 3 → 2 (one line, card-grid.tsx:27)
  • estimateSize must add the bar height — currently assumes width × 1.5 (card-grid.tsx:80)
  • Delete the hover overlay in card-item.tsx:118-165

B: Keep three columns, move controls off the tile

Density is preserved. The tile becomes purely art plus a count badge, and tapping it selects rather than navigates. The stepper lives in a pinned bar with the full 390px to work with.

The measurement

Tile stays ≈ 118px — but nothing has to fit inside it any more. The stepper gets the full width. Constraint dissolved

Trade-offs

  • Rapid-fire: mixed — first card costs 2 taps (select, then +). But the bar persists, so adjusting the same card is fast, and tapping the next card re-targets it.
  • Art forward: strongest — art is the entire tile, uninterrupted.
  • Precise: strongest — the biggest targets of any variant, far from the grid.
  • Cost: indirection. Your eyes are on the grid, the feedback is at the bottom.

Build cost

  • Column count unchanged — estimateSize stays valid
  • New selection state + pinned bar component
  • Bar competes for vertical space with the existing stats sheet trigger (deck-builder.tsx:708)

C: Abandon the grid for full-width rows

Art becomes a thumbnail; name, type and cost carry recognition. Every row is the full 390px, so the stepper has all the space it needs and rows are uniform height.

The measurement

Row is 390px wide, 74px tall. Stepper fits trivially. No conflict

Trade-offs

  • Rapid-fire: strongest — ~9 cards per screen, every + immediately tappable, no selection step.
  • Art forward: fails — a 44px thumbnail is not card art. You'd be reading names, not recognising pictures.
  • Precise: good — wide targets, generous separation.

Build cost

  • Cheapest — uniform row heights are exactly what useVirtualizer wants. Removes the width × 1.5 guess entirely.
  • But it's a different component, not a modified tile — least code reuse with the desktop grid.

Included as the honest baseline: it wins on speed and build cost, and loses on the thing you said mattered.
