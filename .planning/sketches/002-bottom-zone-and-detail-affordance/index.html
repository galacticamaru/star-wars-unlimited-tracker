A: One slot, swapped by state

The bottom holds exactly one bar at a time. No card selected → the stats trigger, exactly as today. Card selected → the action bar takes the slot. Deselect (tap the same card, or the ✕) and stats return.

Bottom zone budget

~56px at all times. No collision — the two never coexist, so nothing had to shrink.

Detail affordance

An ⓘ button in the action bar. It only exists once you've selected a card, which is exactly when "tell me more about this one" occurs. 44px, unambiguous, nowhere near the stepper.

Trade-offs

  • Cheapest vertical cost — grid keeps maximum height.
  • Stats become invisible while adding. You can't watch the count climb toward 50 as you build — which was one of the original complaints about losing deck context.
  • Mitigated by the count chip in the action bar, but that's a smaller signal than a persistent footer.

Tap a card, then tap it again to deselect and watch the bar swap back.

B: Stack both, permanently

Action bar sits directly above the stats trigger. Both are always present, so the deck count stays visible the entire time you're adding — you watch it climb toward 50.

Bottom zone budget

~118px when a card is selected (70 + 48). 16% of the viewport — the grid loses roughly one row.

Detail affordance

Same ⓘ button in the action bar as A. The extra vertical space buys persistent context, not a different affordance.

Trade-offs

  • Never lose deck context — directly answers the original "can't see what I'm building" complaint.
  • Costs a grid row. ~6 cards visible instead of ~9 while a card is selected.
  • Two stacked bars with different border treatments risks looking heavy. Watch whether the bottom feels cluttered.

Note the empty state: with no selection it collapses back to just the stats bar, so the cost is only paid while actively adjusting.

C: One bar that absorbs both jobs

A single 64px bar. Left side is the selected card's identity and stepper; the running deck count lives on the right and doubles as the stats-sheet trigger. With nothing selected the left half shows the deck summary instead.

Bottom zone budget

~64px constant. Near-A cost, near-B context

Detail affordance

Different approach: ⓘ in the tile's own corner plus long-press on the tile. The merged bar is too tight for another 44px button, so the affordance moves back onto the grid — visible at rest, distinct from the tap target, and available before you select.

Trade-offs

  • Tightest use of vertical space while keeping the count visible.
  • The count is doing two jobs — display and sheet trigger. Is that discoverable?
  • Corner ⓘ at 22px is below 44px. Long-press is the real target; the corner icon is the visual hint. That's a deliberate compromise worth judging, not ignoring.

Try the long-press — hold a tile for half a second and watch the fill ring.
